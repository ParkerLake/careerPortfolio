The Marketing Catalog: Unifying Campaigns Into Initiatives

Problem

Marketing ran dozens of campaigns across a dozen+ channels — but every channel identifies its own work differently: a Campaign ID in Google Ads, an SFMC Campaign Tag in email, a Campaign Name in paid social. Stakeholders wanted it all viewed as one initiative; nothing in the data could logically connect it.

Idea & Implementation

I designed and built the Marketing Catalog: every channel-specific execution (an Effort) is logged against a shared Initiative, capturing whatever native identifier that channel actually uses. The catalog generates a standardized UTM Campaign value for each Effort and cleans up UTM Source/Medium into one taxonomy. I built the logging interface and the pipelines that bring it into reporting.

Solution

Reporting groups by Initiative for the full cross-channel picture, or drills into any single Effort — every dollar of web and conversion data traces back to exactly where it came from, no matter which platform's own ID system it started in.

Featured Deep-Dive

The Initiative Goals Manager: Forecasting Before You Launch

Problem

Before a new initiative launched, goals were set from gut feel or last year's number plus a round percentage. Across 6+ brands and dozens of possible channel combinations, that guesswork produced targets that were either sandbagged or unreachable — and indefensible either way once real results came in.

Idea & Implementation

I built a Domo app where a stakeholder configures an initiative — brand, type, channel mix, volume, and how aggressive to be — and Bayesian models trained on that brand and channel's historical performance cascade through the inputs to produce a Low/Med/High estimated range for every metric that matters, from clicks to opportunities.

Solution

Goal-setting became a forecast instead of a guess. Every new initiative now launches with targets grounded in how that exact configuration has actually performed before, not what sounded reasonable in a planning meeting.

NADA: From Vanity to Account-Based KPIs

Problem

Every year, leadership wanted to know if the company's biggest annual conference was worth the investment — but the only numbers on hand were booth traffic and impressions, activity metrics that said nothing about whether the right accounts actually showed up or moved.

Idea & Implementation

I ran the enterprise measurement strategy for four consecutive years — digging into registration, attendance, and account-level data to find what actually predicted pipeline, then presenting the findings and a recommended shift in what to measure to conference leadership and cross-functional stakeholders ahead of every show.

Solution

Each presentation changed what the company measured the following year — four straight upgrades that took the program from counting badge scans to an account-based framework adopted enterprise-wide.
