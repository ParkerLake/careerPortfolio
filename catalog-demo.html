05

Why not just use a naming convention?

A common way to group campaigns is to start every name with a code for the initiative, then text-mine the data for it. That works until someone mistypes the code. Pick a name and see who gets counted.

06

What the ID buys you

One ID, minted once in the catalog, carried by every system that touched the campaign. Two things come out of it.

1. Group what belongs together, across channels

The initiative is assigned in the catalog. Nobody mines names, and nobody maintains exceptions.

2. Credit the specific campaign, not just the channel

Sessions and leads trace back to the email, ad, or post that earned them.

No text mining. Grouping comes from the catalog, not from patterns in names, so a typo can't orphan a campaign.

Reporting gets simpler and optimization becomes possible. You can see which campaign worked, not just that the channel did.

It's the groundwork for multi-touch attribution. Once every touch carries an ID, touches can be joined into a customer's journey.
